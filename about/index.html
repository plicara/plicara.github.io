A personal research practice

plicara is my independent AI research lab. I publish reproducible studies, benchmarks and tools that work on one question: how do we know an AI system works?

Why the lab exists

01. the question

Understand how AI systems behave. Measure what helps.

This research lab is a love letter to the work I enjoy: asking interesting questions, building something to investigate them, and banging my head against the wall until something sticks. Models are one possible outcome, alongside useful tools, datasets and explanations.

The same thread runs through my work inside and outside the lab. I care about systems whose conclusions can be trusted, with honest uncertainty, tiered evidence, and results that survive contact with production.

Who runs it

02. the researcher

I’m Adrian Tame, a mathematician by training and a data scientist by practice. I studied applied mathematics at ITAM and completed the coursework for an MSc in Data Science there. My thesis develops Bayesian Adaptive Spline Surfaces as a surrogate model for Bayesian optimization, an alternative to Gaussian processes.

I work as a data scientist at S&P Global, on document extraction and internal benchmarking for model evaluation. Before that I worked on experimentation and customer segmentation at Zillow, and held data science leadership roles at Coca-Cola FEMSA.

That work led me into AI evaluation, agent interfaces and the tooling around them. plicara is where I explore those questions independently and publish what I find, including the parts that did not work.

Principles

04. how I work

Evidence you can inspect

Methods, code and recorded outputs belong beside the claim. A result should give you a way to check it.

Limits made explicit

A benchmark answers a particular question. Its assumptions, uncertainty and failure cases are part of the result.

Useful baselines

Start with the simplest workable approach. More machinery earns its place by improving an outcome we care about.

Room to be wrong

Negative results and corrections belong in the record. An experiment can be worth sharing even when the proposed idea fails.
